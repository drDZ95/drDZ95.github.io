# Davide Zani

*Psychiatry and psychotherapy trainee · Forensic psychiatry · Biomedical ethics*

I work at the intersection of psychiatry, ethics, law, and human rights.  
My current interests focus on coercion in psychiatry, autonomy, suicide care, forensic mental health, and the ethical implications of predictive and precision psychiatry.

## Research interests

- Psychiatric coercion and human rights
- Suicide, autonomy, and decision-making capacity
- Forensic psychiatry and legal responsibility
- Precision psychiatry and justice
- Ethics of psychiatric risk prediction
- Medical humanities and philosophy of psychiatry

## Current work

I am developing research on how psychiatric coercion is justified, normalised, limited, and potentially replaced by more proportionate and non-coercive clinical approaches.

## Selected themes

### Coercion in psychiatry

I am interested in the gap between formal human-rights principles and everyday psychiatric practice, especially in situations involving suicide risk, involuntary admission, and institutional responsibility.

### Justice-oriented precision psychiatry

Predictive tools may help identify clinical trajectories, but they also risk reinforcing bias, defensive practice, and unequal access to care. My work explores how precision psychiatry could be aligned with fairness, autonomy, and proportionality.

### Philosophy of psychiatry

I am interested in how psychiatric categories shape clinical reality, moral responsibility, and the way suffering is recognised or dismissed.
